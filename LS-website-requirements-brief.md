# LS Smart Machinery website — goals, people, journeys and design requirements

**Working brief · 15 October 2026**

This brief gathers the goals you stated at the start of the website and CMS work and the decisions you made while reviewing the preview. It describes the website LS wants to build. It is not a claim that every item is finished or that unverified business information is ready to publish. Where your later instruction changed an earlier idea, the later decision is the active requirement.

## 1. What the website must achieve

LS Smart Machinery should present itself as a credible Malaysian food-production machinery and engineering partner. A visitor should quickly understand **what LS makes or supplies, which food-production problems it works on, how it plans and supports a line, and how to start a useful conversation**. The site should bring qualified business inquiries and quote or consultation requests from people with real production needs.

The experience must feel like a current international industrial manufacturer: confident, technically clear, visually memorable and easy to browse. It should show the work, people, machinery and production context behind LS. It should not feel like a retail equipment store, a shopping cart, or a generic marketing template. The site can point toward future AI-enabled machinery, but must describe AI as coming only when the capability and timing are confirmed.

Success means a visitor can recognise a relevant food, process, machine, service or customer situation; see enough authentic evidence to trust LS; inspect a machine without losing its shape or brand; and send an inquiry that already carries the context of what they were viewing. Success also means the LS team can update approved content safely without accidental public changes.

## 2. The people using it

| Character | What they come with | What the site must help them do |
|---|---|---|
| First-time explorer | Knows the food or idea, but not the right machinery | Start from food production or the customer journey; understand LS's scope before choosing equipment. |
| Micro business or startup owner | A first product range, limited working space and an evolving process | Explore relevant processes and discuss space, product variety and a realistic first setup with LS. Avoid assuming that one machine is suitable simply because the company is small. |
| SME owner or operations lead | An existing production process with a bottleneck or need to expand | Identify the step to improve, compare relevant machine/process paths and understand installation, training and continued support. |
| Large manufacturer or enterprise team | Multiple machines, line interfaces, procurement and continuity concerns | See line planning, integration, technical detail, service capability and a route to a scoped discussion with LS. Do not promise capacity or performance without a verified record. |
| Plant or production manager | Product requirements, space, operators, uptime and quality concerns | Inspect how equipment works in a production context; review supported specifications and service options. |
| Procurement or supply-chain decision maker | A shortlist, approvals, vendor credibility and delivery questions | Find the exact model, source-backed facts, company identity, contact details and a clear quote route. |
| Existing customer needing support | A machine, a fault, a document or a part | Reach repairs, technical documents or spare parts directly; preserve machine and part details in the handoff. |
| Job candidate | A possible role and questions about working at LS | See professionally presented opportunities and reach the current recruitment listing or LS HR for that role. |
| LS editor, technical reviewer and publisher | Content that changes over time | Prepare, check and publish page content, media, jobs, clients, contact details and translations through distinct responsibilities. |

These are **use situations**, not claims about customer demographics. A company may move between several journeys, and machine suitability depends on the product and process rather than its size label.

## 3. The journeys the public site must support

### Explorer: “What could LS do for my product?”

**Entry:** homepage, search result or food-production navigation. **Path:** a clear LS introduction → one of six concise food choices → authentic examples and the relevant production process → equipment category or solution → contact. The visitor should learn before seeing a long catalogue. Keep the food menu short; the original brief capped the homepage food choices at six and the food navigation at eight simple labels.

### Micro or startup: “How do I begin?”

**Entry:** Customer segments. **Path:** choose the startup segment → see the situation in practical language → explore the food range, process and space considerations → view applicable LS responses → contact LS with that context. Discuss product range and available room. Do not replace guidance with an intake questionnaire or publish unsupported claims about affordability, footprint or output.

### SME or expanding manufacturer: “What is holding production back?”

**Entry:** segment page, food page, machine search or Solutions. **Path:** recognise a bottleneck or planned expansion → see the production steps and possible LS involvement → inspect relevant equipment and service support → request a production discussion. Explain planning, modification, installation and training where documented. Avoid generic recommendations presented as if they were technically approved.

### Large manufacturer: “Can LS support a connected line?”

**Entry:** enterprise segment, Solutions or a specific product. **Path:** see line and facility planning → understand connections between process stages and equipment → review technical records and service scope → arrange a detailed discussion. Show Malaysia as LS's base and documented overseas markets as markets, never as invented offices. Give procurement and engineering readers the detail they need to qualify a conversation.

### Machine evaluation: “Is this model suitable?”

**Entry:** a food page, featured machine, catalogue search or direct model link. **Path:** model-specific introduction and authentic full-machine image → zoom/pan inspection → documented features and food applications → precise technical data and genuine footage when available → related equipment only when the relationship is supported → quote with the model carried into the message. Preserve original machine geometry and KRONO branding. An unresolved image stays unresolved; it must not borrow another model's photograph.

### Service or spare parts: “I need LS to help with equipment I already have.”

**Entry:** Services navigation, a machine page or Contact. **Path:** choose repairs, installation/training, technical documents, production improvement or spare parts → see the practical service scope and direct contact options → send details. Spare-parts requests preserve model, serial or machine number, known article number, part description, quantity, delivery address and remarks. Unidentified-part assistance must retain what the visitor already entered.

### Company, visit or career discovery

**Entry:** Company navigation, homepage story, Locations or Careers. **Path:** authentic company history and work → customer partnership evidence, team or Malaysian base → visit or recruitment action. A visitor should finish knowing how LS developed, what it does today and how to reach the right team. A job applicant should see current-source role destinations and a role-specific HR email, without an undated vacancy being presented as newly posted.

## 4. Information architecture and page requirements

**Primary navigation:** Food production, Machines, Solutions, Services and Company, with Insights, language selection and a clear Portal/contact entry. The page label and dropdown control must be separate, with working hover, keyboard and mobile behaviour. The Machines dropdown keeps the five current public categories — Bakery, Kitchen, Package, Refrigeration Series and Production Line — and shows three featured machines for the active category in a vertical list. Only reveal machines when that category is hovered, focused or tapped. The Services menu shows titles only and fits without internal scrolling.

The initial planning brief also listed nine finer machinery groups, including Cookies & Cake Line, Encrusting Line and Mixing Line. That earlier taxonomy describes possible content groupings; your later direction explicitly keeps **five public menu categories**. Do not add nine top-level categories to resolve the difference.

**Homepage:** strong industrial introduction; “Current at LS” in the overlapping photograph and editorial-panel treatment you approved; a continuous full-width strip of verified featured companies; concise food choices; a linked Customer segments photo/accordion; relevant Insights and customer-partnership material; and a clear contact route. The homepage does not need an introduction-video disclosure, a numbered machine slideshow, recommendation lists beneath the segment panels, or a separate social block.

**Food and segment pages:** start with the visitor's product or production situation. Use real food photographs, process explanation and concise LS responses, then route to relevant categories and contact. All three customer segments need distinct language and journeys. Your latest annotation removes public “recommended machines” lists from both homepage and segment pages; keep helpful discovery links instead.

**Machine catalogue and detail:** retain all 61 entries, five categories, search, Bakery subcategory filtering, 12 results per page, result counts and the visitor's filters/page position on return. Present featured equipment and production processes with an industrial editorial feel. Machine pages need model-specific content and an original-resolution viewer with fit, zoom, pan, reset, keyboard and focus-return controls. Publish specifications, videos, accessories, food outputs and relationships only when they are supported by records. Use a clean text treatment when a photograph cannot be matched exactly.

**Solutions and Services:** show LS's documented planning, equipment, installation, modification, training, maintenance and after-sales work as useful industrial examples. Service pages should build confidence and expose a direct email or WhatsApp action early. Content should answer what LS can help with and what information the visitor should provide.

**Company, History, Partnerships and Locations:** tell LS's real story through verified events and archival imagery; date only verified milestones. Show workshop, installation, training and team material where authentic photographs exist. Use “Customer Partnerships” or similarly professional language rather than invented customer endorsements. Show factual project descriptions and attributed comments only with a source. Locations should show the Balakong base, map, visit action and confirmed hours: Tuesday–Saturday 9:00am–6:30pm; Sunday and Monday closed. The world map may indicate documented markets but must not imply overseas offices.

**Careers and Insights:** present roles from current recruitment/social sources with direct application or HR routes. The legacy website is background reference, not the public destination for new links. Insights needs real LS work, machines and updates, rather than placeholder cards or assumed event claims.

**Footer:** one authentic visible LS logo, full-width information layout, current company registration and legal wording, navigation, usable phone/email/address/hours and verified icon links. Keep the information you explicitly asked to preserve. Social links belong in the shared footer. Your later annotation removed the Insights newsletter block from every footer; do not reintroduce it until there is a real approved subscription flow.

## 5. Inquiry and editing requirements

Ordinary pages use compact **Email** and **WhatsApp** handoffs. Full forms belong on Contact and Spare Parts. Both handoffs must include the machine, originating page and inquiry intent, along with every field the visitor supplied. Require a name, a message or part description, and at least one way to reply; a surname should not be mandatory. Keep entered information available after an external app opens. Opening an email or chat must never be described as delivery confirmation. Careers inquiries go to LS HR.

The CMS edits fixed layouts and content records; it is not a free-form page builder. The agreed workflow is **edit → private live preview → save draft → review → publisher confirmation → public update**. Editors and publishers are separate; machine claims require technical review. Discard, reset and restore prepare or recover drafts without silently changing the published preview. Keep activity history, source references, translation fields, image-match status and featured ordering. English is primary; Chinese and Bahasa Melayu need coherent reviewed versions of the same material, while names, models, numbers and technical meaning remain consistent. The current local CMS is a prototype. Shared storage, real authentication, CRM delivery and production publishing are later infrastructure work.

## 6. Visual and interaction direction

- **Identity:** LS orange, white and black/charcoal are primary. Supporting neutrals may help, but should feel like LS. The goal is a modern European industrial standard: confident, ordered, technical and human.
- **Composition:** full-width dark heroes where approved; substantial yet controlled photography; consistent page gutters; an alternating editorial rhythm; and visible machinery, facilities and people. Keep the current image scale you chose after reviewing the broad reduction. Adjust individual images only when they overflow, distort or hide essential machine geometry.
- **Navigation and content density:** no retail “add to cart” impression, decorative badges, empty placeholders, public editorial notes or duplicated brand blocks. Keep menu labels and paths clear. The footer spans the available width.
- **Motion:** subtle route and scroll movement can add energy. Keep a persistent motion control and respect reduced-motion preferences. Essential content must remain readable when movement is off. Use genuine LS footage for demonstrations; image viewing comes before any future multi-angle or 3D work.
- **Source integrity:** real exact machine and food photography where available; authentic team, workshop and project material; preserved logos and machine proportions; no fabricated testimonials, technical figures, customer relationships or AI capabilities.

**Deferred:** CRM connection, real CMS authentication/shared publishing, any 3D or multi-angle viewer dependent on genuine assets, a live subscription service, production deployment and claims about future AI functionality.

**Needs LS evidence before publication:** unresolved exact machine photos and specifications, configuration-specific food capability lists, more named client logos, customer quotations, fresh job availability, and any conflicting company, legal or technical records.
