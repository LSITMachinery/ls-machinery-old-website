# LS Smart Machinery — proposed homepage improvement plan

Proposal for approval · 16 October 2026 · No implementation performed

The homepage should help a food producer answer three questions: **Does LS handle my production needs? Which equipment or project path should I explore? How do I reach the right person with useful context?** Its current industrial visual identity is a suitable foundation.

## Design direction

Keep the LS logo, orange/charcoal/white identity, straightforward sans-serif typography, authentic factory photography and complete machine silhouettes. Preserve the existing machine scale unless a specific containment defect is demonstrated. Maintain the distinction between equipment photos, food examples and customer/site photography.

Use the inspected [IBM reference](<C:/Users/likto/.agents/skills/awesome-design-md/design-md/ibm/DESIGN.md>) for a disciplined grid, legible information hierarchy, clear action states and restrained surfaces. Use the [DJI reference](<C:/Users/likto/.agents/skills/awesome-design-md/design-md/dji/DESIGN.md>) for named equipment imagery, purposeful detail views and a clear progression from overview to technical information. BMW was considered, but its luxury automotive and configuration emphasis is less useful to LS's consultation-led buying journey.

These are community analyses. Adopt selected principles with LS's colours, type and content. A new LS-specific DESIGN.md can document the approved system later. Brand colour selection and any revision to the current caption-free hero remain explicit design decisions for review.

## Proposed page sequence

| Order | Section | Buyer purpose | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Compact navigation | Reach machinery, solutions, services or contact quickly | Retain familiar labels and Search. Reduce the visual weight of secondary mobile utilities; keep an easy enquiry/WhatsApp path. |
| 2 | Clear hero | Recognise LS and take the next useful step | Keep the current concrete proposition. Retain one principal discovery action and one secondary enquiry action. Keep direct catalogue access as a quiet text alternative. Link the existing machine with a restrained model/application label, subject to approval. |
| 3 | Compact trust evidence | Establish confidence before deeper browsing | Place the two source-backed client records and a concise support/base statement near discovery. Add only approved facts. Static proof is sufficient for the available record count. |
| 4 | Food/process discovery | Start from the buyer's product | Retain the six food families and show an obvious route to all equipment. A guided selector can progressively narrow by food and process when mappings are verified. |
| 5 | Equipment and project pathways | Serve both machine buyers and line-planning buyers | Present category names with a concise process explanation. Distinguish individual equipment exploration from consultation about layouts, integration or production changes. Avoid another large card collection. |
| 6 | Practical LS scope | Explain how the working relationship proceeds | Combine planning, equipment, installation/training and support into one concise sequence with relevant service links. Keep customer size useful as supporting context rather than another long generic section. |
| 7 | One contextual proof section | Substantiate experience | Use a small selection of approved project/customer photographs with a food/process context, LS's role and an appropriate equipment/service link. Use an unlabelled gallery only when that is the strongest available evidence. |
| 8 | Enquiry invitation and footer | Make contact feel clear and manageable | Repeat one direct next step with a short preparation prompt. Explain whether the action sends a request or opens a draft. Retain phone, email, location, hours, support and privacy information. |

Insights can remain a secondary link or a compact relevant item. Combine the current people-focused news tile and subsequent partnership gallery so they contribute one distinct proof story. Avoid repeating the same planning/support proposition across editorial, capabilities and final invitation.

## Guided discovery scope

A proposed Find My Machine flow should start with food/application and process, then optionally ask about production stage or constraints. It should show a small, source-backed shortlist, explain why each item appears and provide “Not sure — discuss my process.” Throughput, utilities or layout inputs should be used only when the catalogue contains reliable matching data.

Keep answers available when opening a product and enquiring. Results are consultation suggestions, not approved engineering configurations or guaranteed suitability. A simpler curated process directory is preferable while mappings remain incomplete.

## Suggested implementation order after approval

First resolve the route mismatch, catalogue naming policy and navigation focus behaviour. Confirm the enquiry operating model. Then approve the homepage structure and token decisions against the existing baselines. Improve priority machine evidence before relying on a new finder. Finally implement the approved composition, verify critical journeys and optimise measured resource costs.

Motion should explain a state change, preserve orientation or confirm an action. Use brief feedback for menus, filters, dialogs and buttons. The proposed homepage does not need pinned scrolling, decorative marquees, background motion or page-blocking reveals.

## Conversion measurement proposal

After privacy and analytics approval, measure discovery starts, application/category-to-detail progression, quote starts, validation errors, handoff clicks and accepted web enquiries as separate events. A WhatsApp or email click is an intent signal; it is not a received lead. Track source route/model without placing message contents or personal details in analytics.

Establish a baseline before setting a numerical conversion uplift target. Follow-up quality and accepted enquiries are more useful business outcomes than page length or CTA clicks alone. No analytics provider was connected during this audit.
